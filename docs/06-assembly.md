# 06. Building the device

The PCB comes from the factory with every SMD part, the key-switch sockets, the power
switch and the module sockets already in place. What is left by hand: 12 LEDs
(24 through-hole joints, the easiest kind of soldering), the module, the switches and
the enclosure.

## 6.1. Order the board

Follow [hardware/pcb/ORDER.md](../hardware/pcb/ORDER.md). The fabrication files are
generated by `hardware/pcb/build.sh` (KiCad 10).

## 6.2. Buy the rest

See the hand-assembled part list in [02-hardware.md §2.8](02-hardware.md#bill-of-materials):
an RP2040-Zero, 4 + 4 + 4 diffused 10 mm LEDs, four MX-compatible switches with
keycaps, M3 hardware and a USB-C cable **with data lines** (charge-only cables look the
same).

Tools: a temperature-controlled soldering iron, 0.8 mm rosin-core solder, flush
cutters, a multimeter with a diode test.

### Check the LEDs on arrival

* Diameter 10 mm, diffused (milky) body, ideally with a flange at the base.
* Long leg = anode (+), short leg = cathode (−), the cathode side of the rim is flat.
* Multimeter in diode mode (red probe on the long leg): red and yellow read
  ~1.8–2.2 V, a 525 nm green ~2.6–3.0 V (a 565 nm yellow-green would read ~2.0–2.2 V,
  like red — that is the wrong part).

## 6.3. Flash the firmware first

Before anything is soldered, check the module itself:

1. Hold **BOOT** on the RP2040-Zero, plug in USB → the `RPI-RP2` drive mounts.
2. Copy `agentlight.uf2` (built per
   [04-firmware.md §4.8](04-firmware.md#48-building-and-flashing)) onto it.
3. The module reboots; its on-board RGB LED shows that the firmware is running.
   `agentlight doctor` (or `screen /dev/cu.usbmodem* 115200` + `HELLO`) should see it.

## 6.4. Assemble the board

1. **Trim the module's header pins** to ~4 mm so the module sits fully in the 5 mm
   sockets, then plug it into J1–J3 on the back side, USB-C towards the rear edge.
2. **Plug the switches** into the Kailh sockets from the front side.
3. **Plug the device in** and run `agentlight test`: buttons should already send
   presses (`agentlight logs -f`).
4. **LEDs go in last**, aligned by the enclosure panel rather than by the board:
   * insert the LEDs into the board's front side by row — red, yellow, green, top to
     bottom; the back side marks `+` (anode, long leg) for every LED;
   * screw the board to the panel so each LED drops into its well up to the flange;
   * solder from the back side, one column at a time, and check each column with
     `RAW` (e.g. `RAW ff ff ff 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00 00` for column 1);
   * trim the legs.

   Without the enclosure, use a flat stand-in panel with 10.3 mm holes on a 20.32 mm
   grid — or just keep the LEDs straight and at an even height while soldering.
5. **Run `SELFTEST`** and tune the colour balance (`LED_CAL_INIT` in
   `firmware/src/config.h`) in the assembled enclosure: the black plastic and the light
   wells change the perceived brightness more than the LEDs themselves.

## 6.5. Install the macOS app

```bash
brew install --cask grisha-gu/tap/agentlight
agentlight install-daemon
agentlight install-hooks
agentlight doctor
```

Or build from source: [host/README.md](../host/README.md).

## 6.6. Troubleshooting

Go in order; do not skip steps.

| Symptom | Check |
|---|---|
| The device is not detected | a cable **with data** lines; try a short cable directly into the Mac |
| `RPI-RP2` does not mount | hold BOOT **before** plugging in USB and for a second after |
| No LED lights up | is the firmware running (on-board RGB LED)? is the device switched on (rear switch)? |
| One LED never lights | polarity (long leg = anode = `+`), then a cold joint — reheat it |
| One LED is always on | a solder bridge to a neighbouring pad |
| A green LED does not light | the transistor (Q1–Q4) under it; 5 V present on the module's `5V` pin |
| A button triggers by itself | the switch is not fully seated in its socket |
| Everything goes dark after 3 seconds | **expected**: the heartbeat watchdog. The daemon is not running or not sending `PING` — `agentlight doctor` |

The universal tool is a multimeter in continuity mode: almost every problem is "is A
connected to B".
